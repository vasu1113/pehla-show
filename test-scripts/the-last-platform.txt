THE LAST PLATFORM
Episode 1: “The Receipt”

Genre: Mumbai-set thriller / family drama
Approximate runtime: 22 minutes

FADE IN:

EXT. DADAR STATION – NIGHT

Rain sweeps across the nearly empty platform. The departure board has been
stuck on “11:52 LOCAL – DUE” for forty minutes.

MEENA SHAH, 31, a school accountant with a canvas tote and a precise way of
standing, waits beneath a leaking tin roof. A small BOY in a yellow raincoat
wanders too close to the edge.

Meena grabs the back of his coat before his mother notices.

MOTHER
Thank you. I only looked away for one second.

MEENA
One second is enough on a platform.

The mother leaves. Meena’s phone BUZZES.

ON SCREEN: “PAPA CALLING.”

Meena watches it ring. Declines it.

Her phone immediately buzzes again — a voice note from her younger brother,
ARJUN.

ARJUN (V.O.)
Didi, call me. Please. Don’t listen to him.

The station lights flicker. Across the tracks, a man in a white shirt watches
Meena from the darkness.

TITLE: THE LAST PLATFORM

INT. LOCAL TRAIN – MOVING – NIGHT

The train finally moves. Meena is alone in her compartment except for an OLD
MAN, 70s, folding newspaper squares into perfect little packets.

Meena calls Arjun. It rings once, then dies.

Her father calls again. This time she answers.

MEENA
Papa?

FATHER (V.O.)
Where are you?

MEENA
On the train. What happened?

FATHER (V.O.)
Get off at Matunga. Do not come home.

MEENA
Why would I get off at Matunga?

FATHER (V.O.)
Because someone has been asking for you.

The line goes dead.

The Old Man notices Meena’s face.

OLD MAN
Bad news?

MEENA
I don’t know yet.

OLD MAN
That is usually the bad kind.

He offers her a newspaper packet. Inside: roasted chana.

MEENA
No, thank you.

OLD MAN
Take it. People make worse decisions hungry.

Meena takes one.

EXT. MATUNGA STATION – NIGHT

Meena steps off. Her father’s familiar car waits outside the gate — but her
father is not driving. A stranger sits behind the wheel.

The stranger opens the passenger door.

STRANGER
Mr. Shah sent me.

MEENA
My father would send Arjun.

STRANGER
Arjun is busy.

Meena backs away. The train begins to pull out. She jumps back aboard just as
the doors close.

INT. LOCAL TRAIN – CONTINUOUS

The Old Man is gone. In his place sits a WOMAN in a green sari, reading a
ledger. She looks up at Meena.

WOMAN
Wrong stop?

MEENA
Wrong car.

WOMAN
No. Right car. Wrong question.

The Woman closes the ledger. Its cover bears the seal of MEENA’S SCHOOL.

Meena freezes.

MEENA
Where did you get that?

WOMAN
Your brother gave it to me.

MEENA
My brother has never seen that book.

WOMAN
Then he is a better liar than your father.

The Woman stands as the train slows.

WOMAN (CONT’D)
Look for the receipt behind the rice tin. Before they do.

She steps off at the next station. Meena tries to follow, but the crowd closes
between them. By the time Meena reaches the door, the platform is empty.

INT. SHAH APARTMENT – KITCHEN – NIGHT

Meena enters through the back staircase. The apartment is dark except for the
blue glow of a television with the sound turned off.

She checks the rice tin. Behind it: an envelope, taped to the wall.

Inside is a receipt for SIX LAKH RUPEES paid to a company called RAINLINE
INFRASTRUCTURE. Her father’s signature is at the bottom.

ARJUN (O.S.)
I told him you would find it first.

Meena turns. Arjun, 24, is soaked, exhausted, and holding a screwdriver like
a weapon.

MEENA
What is this?

ARJUN
The reason you need to leave.

MEENA
That is not an answer.

ARJUN
Papa took money. The school repairs, the rainwater project — all fake bills.

MEENA
Papa handles accounts. He does not build roads.

ARJUN
Exactly. He moves numbers. Other people move concrete.

MEENA
And you knew?

ARJUN
I found out last week.

MEENA
You found out last week and called me tonight?

ARJUN
Tonight they came to the house.

The front-door lock turns.

Arjun kills the kitchen light. Meena presses the receipt against her chest.

FATHER (O.S.)
Meena? Beta, are you home?

Arjun mouths: Don’t answer.

INT. SHAH APARTMENT – HALLWAY – NIGHT

Their FATHER, RAJIV SHAH, 58, enters with a bag of groceries. He looks normal.
Too normal.

RAJIV
Why is the television on if nobody is watching it?

He walks toward the kitchen. Meena steps out before Arjun can stop her.

MEENA
Papa.

Rajiv drops the groceries. An orange rolls across the floor.

RAJIV
You should not be here.

MEENA
That seems to be everyone’s favourite sentence tonight.

She holds up the receipt.

MEENA (CONT’D)
Tell me what I am holding.

Rajiv does not look at it.

RAJIV
Give it to me.

MEENA
Tell me first.

RAJIV
It is not what you think.

MEENA
Then help me think correctly.

Rajiv’s composure breaks for one second.

RAJIV
The school roof was going to fall. The contractor needed an advance. I signed
one paper to keep children safe.

ARJUN
Six lakhs keeps children safe?

RAJIV
You do not understand how quickly things become expensive when people are
afraid.

MEENA
Who is afraid?

The doorbell rings.

Once.

Twice.

Then a calm male voice from outside.

MAN (O.S.)
Mr. Shah. We only need five minutes.

Rajiv whispers.

RAJIV
Do not open that door.

MEENA
You said they came for you.

RAJIV
They came for the receipt.

ARJUN
Same thing.

The doorbell stops. Silence. Then a paper slides under the door.

Meena picks it up. It is a photograph of Meena on the station platform,
taken earlier that night.

On the back: “LAST TRAIN. LAST CHANCE.”

INT. SHAH APARTMENT – LIVING ROOM – LATER

The three sit apart from one another. The television continues to flicker.

RAJIV
There is a locker at the school. The key is in my wallet.

MEENA
What is in it?

RAJIV
Proof that Rainline paid people to certify work they never did.

ARJUN
Then why did you take their money?

Rajiv cannot answer.

MEENA
Papa.

RAJIV
Your mother’s hospital bill. I had one week. Rainline offered a loan. Then a
favour. Then another.

ARJUN
Maa died two years ago.

RAJIV
Debt does not leave just because the person does.

The line lands. Meena looks at the photograph again.

MEENA
We take the locker file to the police.

RAJIV
No police.

MEENA
Why not?

RAJIV
Because one of them is on Rainline’s payroll.

ARJUN
Convenient.

RAJIV
Because it is true.

EXT. SCHOOL COMPOUND – PRE-DAWN

Meena and Arjun climb the locked side gate. Rajiv waits in the car, watching
the empty road.

MEENA
You are not coming in?

RAJIV
Someone needs to see them before they see us.

ARJUN
You mean you need a head start.

Rajiv flinches but says nothing.

INT. SCHOOL OFFICE – PRE-DAWN

Meena opens the locker. Inside: maintenance invoices, photographs of cracked
beams, and a pen drive labelled “MONSOON.”

Arjun finds one more thing: a handwritten note in their mother’s handwriting.

ARJUN
This is from Maa.

Meena reads.

MEENA
“If Rajiv gives you this, it means he finally chose the truth.”

ARJUN
He did not give us anything.

MEENA
Maybe he was trying to.

The office lights turn on by themselves.

Behind them stands the WOMAN IN GREEN from the train.

WOMAN
You took your time.

MEENA
Who are you?

WOMAN
Inspector Leela Deshmukh. Anti-corruption unit.

Arjun raises the screwdriver.

ARJUN
You could have said that on the train.

LEELA
And have you tell your father? I did not know which Shah was still working for
Rainline.

MEENA
Do you know now?

Leela looks toward the window. Outside, Rajiv’s car is gone.

LEELA
I know someone just left.

Meena runs to the window. A single orange sits on the wet pavement where the
car had been.

Inside the orange is a tiny metal key.

ARJUN
Another locker?

MEENA
No. A safety deposit box.

LEELA
At which bank?

MEENA
The bank above Dadar station.

EXT. DADAR STATION – MORNING

The first commuters fill the platform. Meena, Arjun, and Leela race up the
stairs toward the bank entrance.

Meena’s phone rings. “PAPA.”

She answers while running.

MEENA
Where are you?

RAJIV (V.O.)
On the last platform.

MEENA
There is no last platform.

RAJIV (V.O.)
There is when you have spent your whole life avoiding it.

The call cuts. Meena sees Platform 6 across the tracks — the abandoned one,
closed after the monsoon collapse.

On it stands Rajiv. Across from him: the MAN IN THE WHITE SHIRT.

Rajiv holds the pen drive overhead.

MAN IN WHITE
Give it to me and your children go home.

RAJIV
They were always going home. I was the one who got lost.

Meena reaches the barrier. Leela stops her.

LEELA
Wait for my signal.

MEENA
He is my father.

LEELA
Then let him finish choosing.

Rajiv throws the pen drive across the tracks. The Man catches it.

Rajiv smiles — not in surrender, but relief.

The Man plugs the drive into his phone. A video begins to play. His own face
appears, accepting an envelope of cash.

LEELA
Now.

Police move in from both sides. The Man runs. Rajiv does not.

Meena reaches him as handcuffs close around his wrists.

MEENA
Why did you give him the drive?

RAJIV
Because copies are already everywhere.

ARJUN
Where?

Rajiv nods toward the station’s giant departure board. It glitches, then shows
an email confirmation: “FILES SENT TO: PRESS / SCHOOL TRUST / INVESTIGATIVE
UNIT.”

Rajiv looks at Meena.

RAJIV
One second is enough on a platform.

Meena understands: he heard her say it before she saw him.

The 11:52 local finally arrives, twelve hours late.

Meena watches its doors open. Then she turns away from the train and walks
toward her father.

FADE OUT.

END OF EPISODE 1
