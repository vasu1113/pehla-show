ADHOORI BAAT
Episode 1 — "The Return Address"

INT. KITCHEN, MEERA'S HOUSE — MORNING

The kettle. Always the kettle first, before anything else in this house is
allowed to happen.

MEERA
(to no one)
It's on.

She says it every morning. There has been no one to say it to for two years,
and she says it anyway.

The radio next door starts up, the same programme it has been at this hour for
as long as anyone on the lane can remember. A man reading out grain prices in a
voice like a man reading out grain prices.

MEERA
(louder, at the wall)
Turn it down, Kamla-ji.

The radio does not turn down. It never does.

She sets out two cups. Realises. Puts one back. This also happens every morning.

INT. KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS

RAJAN comes down. Nineteen, and wearing it badly. He takes the cup that was put
back and pours from the kettle before it has finished.

MEERA
It hasn't boiled.

RAJAN
It's boiled enough.

MEERA
There's no such thing.

RAJAN
There's this.

He drinks it. It hasn't boiled enough. He drinks it anyway, because he is
nineteen.

MEERA
The landlord came.

RAJAN
When?

MEERA
Thursday.

RAJAN
It's Sunday.

MEERA
I know what day it is.

Neither of them says anything for a while. The grain prices continue next door.

INT. KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS

The house has been in the family since before the mill closed. Meera's
grandfather took it when the company was still parcelling out the lane to men
who could sign their names, and her father held it through the years when
holding anything at all was the whole of a man's occupation, and so it had been
for many years, through the good seasons and the bad ones, and everyone on that
lane knew whose house it was and said so to anyone who asked and to a great
many people who did not.

The plaster in the front room has been coming away from the wall since the rains
before last. There is a way of leaning a chair against it that holds it. Rajan
knows the way. Meera knows the way. Nobody has ever discussed the way.

Kamla next door has a son in Pune and a photograph of him on a shelf where a
television should be. The tailor at the corner will not take work from the house
at the end of the lane and has not given a reason in eleven years. The
municipality repainted the numbers on all the doors in the spring and got two of
them wrong and has not come back.

INT. KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS

In the mornings the light comes through the eastern window at an angle that puts
a bar of it across the second shelf, where the good plates are, which are not
used. In the afternoons it moves to the floor. In the evenings it is gone and
the bulb comes on, and the bulb has been the same bulb since before the rains
before last, which Meera considers a small victory and mentions to nobody.

The lane runs sixty yards from the main road to the wall at the end. There were
eleven houses on it when the mill was open and there are nine now, and of those
nine, four have someone in them who worked at the mill, and of those four, two
will still talk about it. The bus stops at the top at ten past and twenty past
and there is no timetable posted anywhere, and everyone knows this, and no one
has ever written it down.

Water comes between five and seven. Sometimes it comes at five and stops at
half past. On those days people stand in their doorways and look at each other
down the length of the lane and say nothing, because there is nothing to say
about it that has not already been said for years.

INT. KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS

RAJAN
He wants it by the tenth.

MEERA
He always wants it by the tenth.

RAJAN
He said something else this time.

MEERA
Then say the something else.

RAJAN
He said he'd been asked about the house. By someone. He wouldn't say who.

Meera puts down the cup she is holding. It is a small movement and it is the
first thing in this house that has not happened every morning.

MEERA
Asked what about it.

RAJAN
Asked who lived in it. That's all he'd say.

MEERA
And you didn't ask him who was asking.

RAJAN
I did ask.

MEERA
And?

RAJAN
He laughed.

EXT. LANE — MIDDAY

Meera goes to the tailor at the corner, because she has decided that today is
the day she will ask him, after eleven years, why he will not take work from
this house.

She gets as far as the door. He sees her through the glass. He turns around and
goes into the back and does not come out, and after a while she walks home.

She does not tell Rajan. There is a whole category of thing she does not tell
Rajan and it has been growing for two years.

EXT. LANE — LATER

The boy from the post office. Fourteen, on a cycle too big for him, which he
will grow into in about a year and will by then have replaced.

He has one letter. He holds it out and then, oddly, does not let go of it when
Meera takes hold.

MEERA
What.

POST BOY
Nothing.

He lets go. He does not meet her eye. He gets back on the cycle and he is very
careful, going away, not to look back at the house.

INT. KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS

The envelope is old. Not old like a thing kept, old like a thing that has been
somewhere.

MEERA
There's no stamp on this. There's a stamp but it's —

She turns it over.

INT. KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS

RAJAN
What.

MEERA
Nothing.

RAJAN
Ma.

MEERA
It's nothing.

She has gone the colour of the wall.

RAJAN
Whose writing is that?

INT. KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS

MEERA
Mine.

RAJAN
What?

MEERA
It's my writing. It's my hand. That's my hand, Rajan.

RAJAN
You wrote it?

MEERA
Look at the date.

She holds it out to him and he takes it and holds it the way you hold something
that has been dropped from a height.

RAJAN
This is franked eleven years ago.

MEERA
Yes.

RAJAN
Eleven.

MEERA
Yes.

RAJAN
Then why has it —

MEERA
Look at the address.

INT. KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS

He looks at the address. It takes him a moment, because it is written in his
mother's hand and it is perfectly clear and it is a place he has never heard of.

RAJAN
Where is this?

MEERA
I don't know.

RAJAN
It's got our lane. It's got our lane and then a different — Ma, what is
Sirsagaon?

Meera does not answer him.

RAJAN
Ma. Have you ever lived at this address?

MEERA
No.

RAJAN
Then why did you write to it?

MEERA
I didn't.

RAJAN
It's your hand.

MEERA
I know it's my hand.

The kettle, which nobody has attended to, begins to go over. Neither of them
moves to lift it. It goes over and it hisses on the ring and the smell of it
fills the kitchen and neither of them moves.

INT. KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS

RAJAN
Open it.

MEERA
No.

RAJAN
Open it, Ma.

MEERA
I'm not going to open it.

RAJAN
Then I will.

MEERA
Rajan —

He takes it. She lets him take it, which is its own answer, and he gets a finger
under the flap and stops.

RAJAN
It's been opened.

MEERA
What?

RAJAN
It's been opened. Look. It's been opened and it's been stuck down again.

INT. KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS

And from the doorway, where nobody has been standing, and where nobody in this
house has stood for two years, a voice says:

VOICE
I wouldn't.

END OF EPISODE 1
